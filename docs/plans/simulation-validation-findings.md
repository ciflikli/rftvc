## What this does and does not establish

These six checks validate **three different kinds of claims**, not one uniform claim — conflating them was this document's own first-draft error (see review corrections below):

- **DGP/oracle closed-form correctness** (row 1's untested gap aside: rows 2(a)(b), 3(a)(b), 4(a)(b), 5(a), 6(a)): the simulation generators and oracle-stub formulas are independently verified against numerical integration or a direct generator-formula comparison. This validates that the *known truth* used elsewhere is actually correct — it says nothing about any fitted `rftvc` model.
- **Fitted-model prediction error vs. known truth or an external tool** (row 1's relative ISE-vs-baseline check, row 2b's `CompetingRisksForestTV`-vs-truth ISE threshold, row 5's `PEScore`-gap convergence, row 6's ISE-vs-`CoxPHFitter` check): four checks where a fitted `rftvc` model's actual predictions are compared to ground truth, and only row 6 additionally compares to an external tool. Row 2b (Slice 5) closes the CI-gated fitted-model-vs-truth gap for competing risks that row 2's DGP-only checks and the manual bake-off (`bench/s14_cr_sim.py run()`) left open — at default hyperparameters and scenario A only, not the full bake-off's arms/scenarios.
- **Importance-statistic direction and rough magnitude, at two different levels of rigor**: rows 3(e) and 4(e) (S17's original pilot, S19's pilot) check direction/rough magnitude at a **loosened** R=10 pilot margin — not the design's real tight R=50 margin. Rows 3b/3c/3d (Slices 6-8) and 4b (Slice 9) are different and stronger: they run the design's **real** thresholds (3c/3d and 4b's copies/censoring/Markov, unmodified; 3b, freshly recalibrated for its reduced scale) against a fitted forest, in the default CI tier — completing S17's coverage, and closing S19's copies/censoring/Markov scenarios. The one exception is S19's history scenario, whose real magnitude claim (0.25× oracle) is known to fail even at full scale and is not gated by 4b at all (4b substitutes a strictly weaker significance claim instead — see row 4b). None of these — old or new — check that the underlying cumulative-hazard prediction itself is close to truth; that's a different claim (see the bullet above).

**Does not establish:** that every listed check currently passing in CI is sufficient to catch every possible statistical regression. Concretely out of scope:
- **Manual-only gates** (rows #2-#4's R=30/R=50 runs) compute no pass/fail decision in code at all (row 2) or only a loosened pilot version of one (rows 3-4); a regression that only shows up at the real replication count would not be caught by `pytest -m slow` alone, and in row 2's case not by any automated test at all. Rows 2b (Slice 5, competing risks), 3b (Slice 6, trend importance), 3c (Slice 7, timing importance), 3d (Slice 8, competing-risks importance) and 4b (Slice 9, landmark copies/censoring/Markov) close this narrowly for these cases, each at a reduced scale (freshly-calibrated thresholds for 2b/3b; the design's own unmodified rule, verified to transfer, for 3c/3d and 4b's copies/censoring/Markov) — S17's three importance oracle scenarios are now all covered, and so are two of S19's three landmark scenarios (copies, censoring) plus the Markov half of the third. **The one gap left anywhere on this punchlist:** S19's history scenario's real magnitude claim (0.25× oracle) — known to fail even at full scale, not something reduced-scale recalibration can fix (see row 4b) — remains unclosed by design, not by omission.
- **No n-sweep / asymptotic study** beyond Slice 1's two-point (200, 5000) check — general convergence rate claims are not established.
- **No landmark-estimator or non-TVC-static-DGP cross-check against an external tool** — Slice 2's external-tool comparison is TVC-free by construction (`CoxPHFitter` can't handle TVC); no equivalent check exists for the TVC case against, e.g., `CoxTimeVaryingFitter` on a *known-truth* (as opposed to real-data) DGP.
- **No competing-risks cross-check against an external reference tool** on a known-truth DGP (only against `randomForestSRC` via manual R scripts, per `docs/plans/research.md` Q5 — not part of any automated gate). Row 2b (Slice 5) closes the narrower "does the fitted forest get close to truth at all" gap for competing risks, but does not add an external-tool comparison — unlike row 6's `CoxPHFitter` check, there is still no `randomForestSRC`-or-equivalent cross-check in CI for the competing-risks case.
- **No calibration/coverage study** (see `docs/plans/design.md` Part B) — these checks validate point-estimate convergence and relative agreement, not interval coverage.

## Review corrections applied

`codex:rescue` diff review (2026-09-28) caught this document overclaiming in its first draft:
1. The closing section's "every feature's predictions move toward truth" was too broad — competing risks (row 2) and the importance pilots (rows 3-4) don't check fitted-model prediction error at all; corrected by splitting into the three-claim-types structure above.
2. Row 2 called the manual R=30 run a "statistical gate" — it computes no pass/fail decision; corrected to describe it as an unevaluated ISE record.
3. Row 3's pilot description was imprecise about which windows/features Holm correction actually covers and silently omitted that the timing outside-window bound isn't pilot-tested at all; corrected with the exact checks.
4. Row 3's "all oracles checked via numerical integration" was wrong for the competing-risks oracle (checked by direct generator comparison instead); corrected.
5. Row 4's "heavy censoring" statistic is row-level, not subject-level, since the generator emits multiple rows per subject; corrected.
6. Rows 1 and 5's "one-sided 95% CI" bounds are actually `t.ppf(0.975)`-based, i.e. 97.5% one-sided bounds (more conservative, not a statistical error, just a labeling imprecision inherited from the underlying test files' own docstrings); corrected.
